## What changed my approach

My 77% pixel figure against the owner's 60% meant I was measuring the wrong things. Looking at
the WHOLE page instead of the worst-disagreeing crop immediately showed defects no check of mine
had reported — because every comparison I had built compares things that exist in **both**
documents, and a missing or duplicated element has no counterpart to compare.

## Borderlines — the owner's first point

Measured: R10.026's original draws **119** cell-sized boxes on page 1; the build drew **319**,
with 12 boxes stroked twice. R10.034: 171 against 264.

I got the cause wrong twice before finding it, and the record matters:

1. **Removed pens from 21 rectangles** → rendered stroke count 117 before, **117 after**.
2. **Removed the box from text styles where a rectangle already drew one** → **117 again**.
3. Stopped hypothesising and looked at one duplicated box (x 23..200 y 141..157). The JRXML
   contained that element **literally twice**. `r10_polish.py`'s info-border step ran twice —
   once before the header block was reverted and re-applied, once after — and its
   "already present" test compares against the rectangles that existed when the band was READ,
   not against the ones it is adding.

**45 duplicate rectangles removed** across the eight files; R10.026's doubly-stroked boxes went
from 12 to **0**.

## Background colour — the owner's second point, same root

The original separates its header cells with **gaps**, not strokes:

```
original fills   x 24..102 | 103..202 | 204..329 | 331..446
                        ^1pt      ^2pt      ^2pt          <- the white page IS the separator
```

The build carried **two overlapping sets** of navy rectangles — one at the original's widths and
one at the widths the report was authored with. The wide leftovers bridged the gaps, so the
header rendered as one solid navy expanse with no cell separation. `r10_hdr_merge.py` had set the
header CAPTIONS' x/width from the original but left the rectangles behind them alone.

Fixed by deleting rectangles whose (x, width) matches no cell in the original: 17–23 per
landscape report. I first tried *snapping* each stale rectangle to the cell it overlapped most —
wrong, because a narrow cell overlaps the wide `ACQ` group cell more than its own, so ten
distinct cells collapsed onto one. Reverted and replaced with deletion, which has no pairing to
get wrong.

## A regression I introduced and caught

Between those two, I wrote a pass that removed a rectangle's pen wherever the build stroked a box
the original did not. It removed **real** borders: the build's span-both header cells are
full-height while the original strokes per sub-cell, so "unmatched" meant "different cell
structure", not "should not exist". Caught by looking at a colour crop — the header's vertical
separators had vanished — and reverted by re-running the border-colour pass.

## R10.034 now renders 12 pages

The single largest difference, and not a layout matter at all: the build produced **one** page
against the original's **twelve**.

- **Data**: read out of the report's own reference PDF — 11 buyers with values plus one blank
  page, which is what the original's 12th page is (4 spans: title and footer only). Keyed by
  (row label, column x-range) taken from the JRXML's own layout. Three earlier keying attempts
  failed and are documented in the scripts: by value position (31 of 44 defaults are values like
  `0` appearing nineteen times), by value with row label (same), and by column CENTRE (a
  left-aligned value sits at x-centre 241 in a cell whose centre is 367).
- **Structure**: statement moved from `title` into `detail`, `$P{}` → `$F{}`, grouped on the
  buyer with `startNewPage="true" resetPageNumber="true"` so each gets its own page and its own
  "Page 1 of 1" — which is what the original prints. Group syntax copied from R07.017–R07.019 in
  this corpus rather than assumed.
- **Query**: a dummy `SELECT ... FROM DUAL UNION ALL ...` carrying those twelve rows, so the
  column mapping is explicit and can be swapped for the real query. The Java harness fills from a
  matching map datasource, and JasperReports ignores the query when given one — so the mapping is
  right and the report still previews without a database.

**Result: 1 page → 12 pages.** Mean agreement across all twelve: **52% → 64.5%**.

## A finding I cannot explain, and will not guess at

The original's own pages do not share one vertical offset:

| | pages 1 and 5 | the other nine |
|---|---|---|
| `Date of Issuance` | y **138.5** | y **174.5** |
| title | y 53.2 | y 53.2 |

A 36pt difference. It is not driven by content height — pages 1 and 3 have identical statement
heights (579.3) and different starts; nor by the bottom of the content, which varies from 688 to
754. Every earlier round compared page 1 only, so the whole layout had been tuned to the offset
used by **two pages out of eleven**.

Shifting the statement down 36pt to match the other nine took pages 2–11 from ~50% to **72–75%**
and page 1 from 83% to 51% — mean 52% → 64.5%, so it stays. **Do you know why pages 1 and 5 sit
36pt higher?** If those two buyers have a section Crystal suppresses, that is reproducible and
worth another few points.
